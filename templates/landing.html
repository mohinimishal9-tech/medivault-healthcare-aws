What happens to one patient record

From the moment a nurse saves a registration to the moment a doctor opens it.

  1. A signed-in user submits the record

    The app checks the user's IAM role before anything is written.

  2. KMS issues a one-time data key

    A new 256-bit key is generated under the customer managed key. Only its encrypted copy is kept.

  3. Sensitive fields are encrypted

    Name, ID number, phone, address and notes are sealed with AES-256-GCM. Age, gender and diagnosis stay readable for analytics.

  4. Aurora and S3 store only ciphertext

    Structured data goes to Aurora. Scans and lab reports go to S3 with SSE-KMS.

  5. CloudTrail and Config keep watching

    Every decrypt is logged, and Config rules flag any setting that drifts out of policy.

Six AWS services, one job each

Amazon Aurora
Stores patient and admission records in an encrypted cluster inside private subnets.
Amazon S3
Holds lab reports and scans. Versioned, encrypted, and blocked from public access.
AWS KMS
Owns the master key and rotates it every year. Each decrypt needs a permitted role.
AWS IAM
Gives each role the least access it needs. Administrators cannot decrypt patient data.
AWS Config
Checks seven security rules continuously and flags anything out of compliance.
AWS CloudTrail
Records who called which API, from where, and whether it was allowed.
